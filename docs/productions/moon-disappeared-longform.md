## Narration

### 01 — An Empty Sky
Imagine stepping outside tonight, looking up, and finding an empty sky. Not a cloud covering the Moon. Not a new moon hidden in darkness. The Moon itself has vanished, as if it never existed. At first, the world might seem normal. Streetlights glow, cars pass, and the Earth keeps turning. But we have just lost something that quietly shapes our oceans, our nights, and even the long-term stability of our planet.

### 02 — A Darker World
The first thing you would notice is darkness. The Moon does not create light; it reflects sunlight. Take it away, and moonlit nights disappear. Away from cities, stars and the Milky Way could look more spectacular on clear nights. But animals that depend on moonlight to hunt, hide, navigate, or reproduce would have to adapt. For some nocturnal creatures, a brighter moon is a useful spotlight. For others, darkness is protection.

### 03 — The Ocean Reacts
Now look at the ocean. The Moon's gravity stretches Earth's oceans into tidal bulges. As the planet rotates, coastlines move through these changing water levels. If the Moon suddenly vanished, those lunar forces would disappear. But the ocean would not magically freeze in place, and every beach would not be hit by a giant wall of water. Water is already moving, so tides would readjust over time, with local effects depending on coastline and seafloor shape.

### 04 — The Sun Remains
The Sun also raises tides. Even though it is much farther away, its gravity still pulls differently across Earth. Without the Moon, solar tides would remain, but they would generally be smaller than today's familiar combination of lunar and solar tides. That shift would affect tidal flats, estuaries, coastal feeding grounds, and the timing of life along the shore. Entire ecosystems have evolved around the regular rhythm of rising and falling water.

### 05 — Nature's Clock
Think about the animals that use the Moon as a clock. Some corals synchronize spawning with lunar cycles. Certain sea creatures time their behavior around tides. Migrating animals may use moonlight as one cue among many. Remove the Moon, and those cues change. It would not mean all wildlife suddenly dies. Nature is more complicated than that. But species already struggling with climate change and habitat loss would face yet another disruption.

### 06 — The Planet's Balance
The biggest consequences might take much longer to reveal themselves. Earth's axis is tilted, and that tilt gives us seasons. The Moon helps limit how much the axis wobbles over very long timescales. Without it, Earth's orientation could become less stable, potentially bringing more dramatic shifts in seasonal patterns over geological time. But that is not a disaster that unfolds tomorrow morning. The first sunrise would still come. Summer would not instantly become winter.

### 07 — Clocks and Eclipses
There are smaller changes too. The Moon has long helped slow Earth's rotation through tidal friction. Remove that influence, and this particular braking effect is reduced, though the length of a day would not suddenly change. And say goodbye to familiar solar and lunar eclipses. With no Moon to cross the Sun, or pass through Earth's shadow, those celestial shows would no longer happen in the ways we know them.

### 08 — A Quiet Partner
So, would Earth survive without the Moon? Yes. The oceans would remain. The Sun would still rise. Gravity would still hold us to the ground. But the world would be darker at night, coastal rhythms would change, countless species would have to adjust, and Earth's future climate could become harder to predict. The Moon is more than a beautiful object in the sky. It is a quiet partner in the story of life on Earth. What would you miss most?
